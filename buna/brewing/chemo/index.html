Chemo

What it is Coffee leaf cooked with herbs and spices.

Chemo is a hot Arabica leaf beverage from Tepi, Southwestern Ethiopia, made with fresh or sun-dried leaf, a paste of herbs and spices, often butter, and salt.

Start Boil the leaf, then cook on with the paste.

In the common roasted route, tender leaf tips are roasted and crushed, boiled about 15 minutes, then cooked 25 to 30 minutes more with the herb and spice paste, in about a litre of water. Salt balances the bitterness. It is filtered and served hot.

Routes Roasted, heated, Majang.

Roasted is the most common and gives a sweeter, more balanced cup. Heated tips, warmed briefly over fire, give a milder one. In the Majang route leaf and chilli are boiled separately and filtered together.

Notice Basil, koseret, lemongrass, fennel, ginger, garlic, onion.

Mint, rue, coriander and korarima are also named. The set changes by household.

Source Tepi ethnobotanical study.

Awoke, Gizaw and Tilahun, Traditional preparation and cultural significance of chemo, an indigenous coffee-leaf beverage in Southwestern Ethiopia, Journal of Ethnobiology and Ethnomedicine, 2026.
